# 06 Enterprise AI Governance: full walkthrough, one take

Runtime 4:49. AI processing shown at 10x; everything else is real time.

**[0:00]** This is the Enterprise AI Governance system: every AI system in the company on one tamper-evident record, assessed by agents against a swappable rulebook, with humans owning every verdict. This is the whole loop in one sitting.

**[0:05]** Lucy, a governance reviewer, signs in: the first of the three accounts this film uses.

**[0:10]** The control tower. The whole AI estate on record, tiered by risk under the live policy pack, and every count on this page computed from the register, not typed into a slide.

**[0:15]** Below the tiers, an executive dashboard nobody typed: systems in production, open governance issues, policy violations, the compliance score, all measured off the record. And under that, the register itself.

**[0:21]** Type, and the estate filters live. Every row here is a system somebody owns.

**[0:25]** Watch a new AI system come under governance. There are two doors in: fill a form, or describe it in a sentence.

**[0:28]** The form first, for when you already know the answers.

**[0:32]** Name, owner, purpose, what data it touches, who checks it.

**[0:33]** Register and score. The policy rules score it instantly, and the notice is honest about the price: no model call.

**[0:38]** The second door: plain words, and an agent does the paperwork.

**[0:40]** One paragraph, sped up here: what it does, who owns it, what it reads, who checks it.

**[0:43]** One click. An agent reads the paragraph, fills the registry record field by field, and kicks off the full assessment pipeline against the active rulebook.

**[0:46]** And there it is on the estate: CreditLens, on the record, assessment running. The register just ticked up by two.

**[0:49]** Open CreditLens while the assessment is still running, and the record page narrates the work: which rulebooks apply, what each inspector is checking.

**[0:54]** Several minutes of agent work, sped up ten times.

**[1:12]** And it lands exactly where a credit scorer reading age and postcode should land: high risk, with findings.

**[1:17]** The findings tab. Each one names the control it violates, the inspector that raised it, the evidence, and a concrete fix.

**[1:21]** A slow read down the list: nothing here is a vibe, every finding traces to a rule in the active policy pack.

**[1:27]** Findings are the machine's opinion. Every verdict that matters is a human's, and there are four human moves on this page.

**[1:30]** First: confirm a finding is real. Approved, attributed to the reviewer.

**[1:33]** Second: overrule the machine.

**[1:34]** An override demands a written reason, because it lands in the audit trail. Sped up.

**[1:35]** Overridden, on the record, with the reviewer's name on it.

**[1:38]** Third: routing. An agent reads the finding, picks the right desk, sets an owner and a deadline from severity.

**[1:40]** A real agent call, sped up. Routed, and the routing itself is on the audit chain.

**[1:45]** And fourth: humans raise findings too. A pen test result gets logged straight onto the asset: what is wrong, how severe, and how you know, filled in a blink here.

**[2:05]** Logged. It joins the same chain as the agent findings, attributed to the person who raised it, and lands on the remediation board like any other.

**[2:10]** One more human power, and it leaves a mark. The framework put CreditLens at high risk. This reviewer thinks that undersells it: declines only get a human look once a month.

**[2:12]** A tier correction demands a written reason, sped up here.

**[2:13]** The human's tier now stands, above the machine's. The machine scores, the person decides.

**[2:17]** And the measurement tab, for what the model actually does in production. Fairness is a property of decisions, not descriptions.

**[2:19]** The owner drops a month of real scoring decisions as a CSV: age group, prediction, outcome.

**[2:23]** Every number on this panel is computed from that file on the spot, including the approval gap between the two groups. No pipeline, no numbers taken on faith.

**[2:28]** The second account: Omar, who owns remediation.

**[2:36]** Every open finding across the estate lands on the remediation board, and the header counts the truth: total findings, overdue, unassigned.

**[2:41]** The whole estate as one list: severity, finding, asset, owner, deadline, status.

**[2:45]** The overdue view is one click away, which is what makes the governance real rather than decorative.

**[2:48]** And the unassigned queue, where new findings wait for an owner. The pen test finding a human logged a moment ago is here. Watch it get worked.

**[2:52]** It gets an owner, and leaves the unassigned queue for Omar's list.

**[2:55]** On Omar's own board now.

**[2:58]** Dragged into progress, and the status change is written back, not just painted.

**[3:03]** Closing a finding takes proof, not a promise.

**[3:05]** The remediation evidence attaches to the finding itself.

**[3:08]** And closed, with an owner, a paper trail, and a file that proves the fix. Agents raise, people remediate, the record holds it all.

**[3:13]** The third account: the administrator, who owns the rulebook itself.

**[3:21]** The rulebook is a first-class object. The live policy and framework packs are named right here, and the administrator can swap them.

**[3:27]** Swap the policy pack.

**[3:29]** The entire estate re-scores against the new rules on the spot. Zero code changed. That is what a swappable rulebook means.

**[3:30]** For the person who will never open a finding, the executive brief.

**[3:32]** An agent reads the estate scorecard and writes the state of AI governance in plain sentences. The register, the findings and the audit trail also export as CSV for auditors.

**[3:35]** And governance does not sleep: the nightly sweep re-checks monitored assets, each one a real model call.

**[3:37]** Minutes of monitoring, sped up ten times: how many checked, and whether anything new surfaced. This runs on a schedule with nobody watching.

**[4:22]** And the pack swaps back just as easily.

**[4:25]** One last stop on the CreditLens record.

**[4:29]** Underneath it all, the audit trail: every pipeline step, every finding, every routing, every human decision, hash-chained in order.

**[4:33]** A slow read down the chain: each entry carries the hash of the one before it, so tampering anywhere breaks the chain visibly. The agents' work and the humans' judgment sit in one record.

**[4:39]** Back at the tower, the tiles have moved since we arrived, because everything we just did was real: registrations, findings, routings, remediations.

**[4:44]** That is the loop: agents assess and route, people decide and remediate, and the record proves all of it.
